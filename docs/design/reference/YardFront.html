Audience

For people who
don't want to guess.

For consumers

Before you list, buy, or give away.

A photo is cheaper than regret. Check what your thing is worth before the garage sale, before the lowball offer, before you hand it to the thrift store.

  • Estate cleanouts. — Price a house full of belongings in an afternoon.
  • Moving sales. — Know which piece of furniture is the $40 one and which is the $1,400 one.
  • Thrift & resell. — Scan shelves in-store; surface the arbitrage.
  • Inherited collections. — Cameras, records, coins, watches — without becoming an expert.

For businesses

A single API. Every category.

One REST endpoint returns a priced, confidence-scored estimate for any item your customers or operators photograph. Fair pricing, at scale, without a category specialist on payroll.

  • Estate sale companies. — Price entire inventories overnight, consistently.
  • Thrift chains. — Replace guesswork at the intake counter with a number.
  • Insurance appraisers. — Back every claim with comparable sales, not a spreadsheet.
  • Resale platforms. — Suggest fair list prices the moment a seller uploads.

For developers

One endpoint.
Every category.

POST an image URL, receive a priced, sourced, confidence-scored estimate in under three seconds. Typed SDKs for TypeScript, Python, and Go. Pay per successful lookup.

  • REST & streaming endpoints — poll, or subscribe to incremental results as marketplaces return.
  • Provenance included — every estimate ships with the underlying sold-for sample it came from.
  • Webhook replays — re-run estimates when new comps arrive, no extra charge.
  • SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and rate-limited by keys; enterprise SLAs available.
POST · /v1/appraise
// TypeScript · @yardfront/sdk
import { YardFront } from "@yardfront/sdk";

const yf = new YardFront({ apiKey: process.env.YF_KEY });

const estimate = await yf.appraise({
  image_url: "https://cdn.acme.com/lot-7412.jpg",
  context: { zip: "98103", condition: "good" },
  marketplaces: ["ebay", "chairish", "1stdibs"],
});

console.log(estimate.range, estimate.confidence);
